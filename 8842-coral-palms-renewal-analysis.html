Section Two

Three things that cap the upside

It is the smallest unit in the entire survey

At 1,168 square feet, your unit is smaller than all nineteen comparables. The next smallest is 1,203, and the leased figures above belong to homes between 1,203 and 1,249 square feet.

No garage

Street parking only, where several competing units offer assigned or covered parking. It is a small thing on paper and a real one to a renter comparing two listings side by side.

Section Three

On the 2024 rent and the $1,650 comparable

The $1,595 achieved in 2024 was real, but the market has moved. Orlando-area rents are down roughly 4% year over year, and the clearest evidence is in your own complex: 8846 Coral Palms asked $1,600 this August and had to accept $1,500.

On the $1,650 listing, the unit we can locate at that price is 8836 Dunes Court in Legacy Dunes, not Coral Palms. It is 1,249 square feet, in a gated community, and includes grounds care, internet and pool maintenance — a materially different product. If you have a different unit in mind, send us the address and we will pull it.

Asking prices are not clearing in this pocket. Units currently listed between $1,595 and $1,800 have been on the market 69 to 232 days without leasing. The average active listing has sat 123 days.

Section Four

Why we would stop at $50

$600 vs. $1,340

The increase produces $600 across twelve months. A single vacant month costs $1,340, before make-ready, cleaning and the cost of placing a new resident. It would take more than two years of the increase to recover one month empty.

Your resident pays on time and the unit is occupied. That matters more here than it would elsewhere, for two reasons.

The long-term renter pool in this corner of 34747 is genuinely thin. The area around Coral Palms is dominated by vacation and short-term rental product, which means fewer conventional long-term renters are shopping here than the raw listing count suggests.

And the no-pet restriction narrows that pool again — by $150 and nearly three months, on the evidence above.

A $50 increase is a fair correction that a good resident will accept without much thought. A larger one invites them to start looking — and in this submarket, replacing them is neither quick nor cheap.

Section Five

Summary and recommendation

  • $1,290 is below market. The increase is justified and we support it.
  • The closest comparable — same complex, also no pets, 52 sq ft larger — asked $1,600 and leased at $1,500 in August.
  • Comparable leases in the area range $1,450 to $1,650, with the top figures going to larger, gated, or utility-inclusive units.
  • Your unit is the smallest in the nineteen-property survey, has no garage, and cannot accept pets.
  • The pet restriction alone is worth $150 a month and 77 extra days on the evidence from an identical floor plan.
  • The $50 increase returns $600 a year. One vacant month costs $1,340 plus turnover expense.

Our recommendation: renew at $1,340

It is a fair, well-supported correction that keeps a reliable resident in place. If the renewal goes smoothly, we can take another measured step next year and move toward market across two cycles rather than risking a vacancy to get there in one.
